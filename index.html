01 Full-stack · Next.js · 2026

Enterprise operations portal · Client: GIPL

An operations portal for GIPL’s wagon leasing business

A role-based portal that replaces spreadsheet and email tracking for procurement, bank guarantees, wagon pricing, maintenance and reporting. A five-developer team built it on Next.js, React, TypeScript, Prisma and PostgreSQL, with a Salesforce integration through MuleSoft.

My role
Full-stack developer. I was the primary developer of the Wagon Pricing and Wagon 360 modules and the main contributor to the Bank Guarantee module.

  • Wagon Pricing & RFQs

    Requests for quotation, supplier quotes and revisions, L1–L5 quote comparison, an MIS dashboard and reminders. I built the UI, API routes, service layer and migrations.

  • Bank Guarantee lifecycle

    A four-stage workflow with parallel Finance and Legal review, drafts kept separate from amendments, a server-stamped completion date and pre-expiry reminders.

  • Wagon 360

    Search one wagon and see its design, rake, contracts, guarantees, overhaul schedule, warranty and documents on one screen, assembled from eight modules.

  • Next.js
  • React
  • TypeScript
  • Tailwind CSS
  • Prisma
  • PostgreSQL
  • Zod
  • Azure Service Bus
  • MuleSoft
  • Docker

Context

The client’s Operations, Finance, Legal and IT teams tracked wagon procurement, guarantees and supplier quotes in spreadsheets and email. The portal gives each team role-based screens, an audit trail of field changes, and module owners who review and accept their module.

Before development started, I led eight in-office requirement-gathering sessions with GIPL’s stakeholders and documented the agreed Phase-1 scope, including what was out of scope, through to full sign-off.

Every module follows the same layers, which kept five developers’ code consistent:

  2. API route/api/v1/…
  3. Servicebusiness rules · Zod
  4. RepositoryPrisma

Salesforce MuleSoft Azure Service Bus Pricing service requests in · selected quote back

Problems I solved

  1. Dates that shift across timezones

    Quote validity uses a 15-day “expiring” window and a 30-day comparison grace window. Date-only rules are easy to get wrong between UTC and IST, so I compared exact calendar-day boundaries. A quote’s status no longer depends on timezone conversion.

  2. Revisions as records, not edits

    A revised quote is saved as a new record. Ranking uses each supplier’s latest revision, and earlier revisions stay available for audit.

  3. Values that can’t drift

    Some Bank Guarantee values are computed when they’re read instead of being stored, so they can’t fall out of sync with the data they depend on. The workflow’s stage model was revised several times as client reviews refined the requirements.

02 Approach

From the first meeting to the last review.

I’m most useful in the gap between what a client describes and what the code has to do. Here is how a feature usually moves through my hands, with a real example from the work above at each stop.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Run requirement sessions with stakeholders and agree on what is in scope and what isn’t.

    e.g. led eight sessions and the Phase-1 scope sign-off for GIPL.

  2. 02

    Model

    Break a workflow into states, rules and data before writing screens.

    e.g. Bank Guarantee stages with parallel Finance and Legal review.

  3. 03

    Design

    Prototype screens and flows in Figma so the client reacts to something concrete.

    e.g. HDU and Pathkind prototypes.

  4. 04

    Build

    UI, typed APIs, validation and exports, with loading and error states handled.

    e.g. the Wagon Pricing module, end to end.

  5. 05

    Review

    Demo to module owners, turn findings into fixes, and go round again.

    e.g. led review demos for Pricing and Bank Guarantees.

  6. 06

    Explain

    Put technical constraints into plain language for business and integration teams.

    e.g. walking the client’s integration team through the quote model.
